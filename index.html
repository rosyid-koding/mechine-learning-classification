Step 0: What is classification?

Classification means sorting something into a category. The computer looks at clues and puts the thing in one box.

Classification asks "which category?" A different kind of ML, regression, asks "how much?" (for example, predicting the exact hours someone sleeps). Our project is classification.

Step 2: Missing values

A computer can't do maths on an empty cell. In our data, 219 of 374 people have a blank Sleep Disorder. Each dot is one person. What should we do?

The notebook fills blanks with "None": a blank means "no sleep disorder". Deleting would throw away more than half the people and leave nobody healthy to learn from.
What we'll type in Colab

df.isnull().sum() counts blanks. fillna('None') fills them.

Step 3: Features and label

Features (x) are the clues. The label (y) is the answer we want to predict. Some columns should be thrown away. Sort every column.

Memory trick: x = what we know, y = what we want to find out.
What we'll type in Colab

x = df.drop(columns=[...]) keeps the clues. y = df[['Sleep Disorder']] keeps the answer.

Step 4: Turn words into numbers

Computers only do maths, so words must become numbers. Don't write Nurse=1, Teacher=2: that creates a fake ranking. Instead, make a yes/no (1/0) column for each category. This is one-hot encoding.

Switch it and watch the table.

pd.get_dummies(x, drop_first=True) encodes the features. y.map(label_mapping) encodes the label.
